A Comparative Analysis of the “X-ing of Y” Construction in Conservative and Progressive Texts

Tava Reese · Department of Linguistics, CU Boulder

LING 5430 · Dr. Laura Michaelis · May 2025

Though nominalizations are ubiquitous in modern English, they are especially frequent in scientific, political, and religious texts. Fowler emphasized that they exist as strategic, grammatical mechanisms for persuasion and the transmission of beliefs. This study compares one nominal construction, "X-ing of Y," in the texts of two very different organizations: Focus on the Family (FOTF) and the Gay and Lesbian Alliance Against Defamation (GLAAD).

TL;DR

Both FOTF and GLAAD use gerundive nominalizations at similar overall rates, but their usage of mental space types within "X-ing of Y" constructions differs significantly. FOTF overwhelmingly favors actual space, aligning with its emphasis on tradition, religious authority, and moral certainty. GLAAD exhibits a near-even split, with a slightly greater tendency toward virtual space.

01Why nominalization matters

Fowler argues persuasively that nominalization is frequently used to obscure agency and to reify processes as facts/referential entities. This creates new, lexicalized terms that serve to "control through the one-way flow of knowledge." In light of this, working towards a more accurate understanding of nominalization benefits all of society, because it can provide insight into how politicians, media outlets, and different ideological communities exert influence by portraying "the world as unconsciously organized in the mind."

I did not create transphobia, therefore, I cannot be responsible for the undoing of it. GLAAD
But it only took 90 years to get to Abraham Lincoln from the writing of the Constitution. FOTF

Although FOTF and GLAAD represent opposing moral ideologies, I hypothesized that both would use a similar percentage of overall gerund/gerundive nominalization due to their shared goals of persuasion, belief reinforcement, and moral framing. However, I also expected the usage of the "X-ing of Y" construction to be split across the two corpora, in ways that align with the ideological concerns of the organizations: virtual space more frequent in GLAAD, actual space more frequent in FOTF.

02Two kinds of gerund

Gerunds, a specific type of -ing nominalization, are commonly understood to straddle the boundary between "action" and "fact." Under a newer framework, there are two broad types. First, the gerund that behaves like a noun phrase, the Nominal Gerund (NG). These can take adjectives and determiners and are often followed by an of-phrase. The second type behaves more like a clause and can only be modified by adverbs: the Verbal Gerund (VG), or gerundive.

It has been noted that VGs can also be thought of as "process gerundives" and NGs as "fact gerunds." As Heyvaert puts it, "While nominals can designate or encode actions… they cannot be said to encode facts. Rather, they are USED in factive SLOTS or CONTEXTS."

03Anatomy of the "X-ing of Y"

This construction is characterized by an -ing nominal (the gerund), followed by the preposition of. It cannot take direct objects or adverbs like its verbal relatives, making it most at home in the nominal gerund category. Click each part.

04Mental spaces, in three dimensions

Many further subtypes of nominal and verbal gerunds can be made. One such distinction is found within the realm of mental spaces. There are two dominant mental spaces in the literature: actual and virtual. Actual space refers to historical, presupposed, or real events that may be completed or relevant to the current context. These spaces are more "fact-like" and are typically used in reference to a real or presupposed agent. Virtual spaces deal more with hypothetical, counterfactual, or future events/outcomes.

Nominal gerunds "have… become less common in contexts that project future or hypothetical worlds and… future or hypothetical events." This leaves the virtual spaces to the VGs, who already prefer more abstract or progressive situations. Therefore, the commonly nominal "X-ing of Y" construction is a good test case for the claim that overtly religious organizations will use more nominal forms of gerunds than progressive advocacy groups.

05Two corpora

Two original corpora were created for this analysis using SketchEngine: around 500,000 tokens from the Focus on the Family website, and around 500,000 from the GLAAD website. These two organizations were selected due to their starkly different ideological goals, as well as their comparable positions of prestige and influence in their respective spheres. Rather than contrasting against lay speech, this study contrasts two media outlets against one another.

06Results

Both groups use almost the same percentage of gerund and gerundive nominalizations, as well as similar proportions of "X-ing of Y" constructions. However, the use of actual mental space with the "X-ing of Y" construction was far more common in the FOTF corpus than virtual mental space, with only 6% of the tokens using virtual space. GLAAD's usage contains an even split between actual and virtual space, with a slight preference for virtual.

07Discussion

These findings seem to support the idea that a more progressive, advocacy-oriented organization like GLAAD concerns itself less with grounding its ideas in past, traditional "truths" of the sort that FOTF presupposes. Anchoring speech in the past or drawing the past into the present through factual/actual mental spaces is a mechanistic attempt to accomplish the goals that FOTF espouses. Because GLAAD is an organization focused on changing and challenging harmful norms, it follows that their mental points of reference would exist in the future.

One of the most striking things about this analysis is how simultaneously different and similar the content is in both corpora. Human rights issues, abortion, health and safety, children, media, love and relationships, self-actualization, politics, and sexuality all featured prominently in both. The overlapping vocabulary threw the differing composition of the utterances into harsher relief. The word understanding is a good example: it floats back and forth between virtual and actual space depending on the context.

When FOTF's rare virtual "X-ing of Y" constructions did occur, they were typically employed to refer to an undesirable or unlikely prospective future, or when the speaker wished to cast an event as purely hypothetical.

08Conclusion

If the popular saying "the medium is the message" is to be believed, then this study should inspire a sober examination of the kind of allowances and trust we give to ideological organizations, even when we are inclined to agree with their mission. Though CDA characterizes the choice of grammatical mechanisms as strategic and manipulative, we need not understand the different grammatical tactics shown here to be malicious. Billig argues that even the analysts and theorists developing these most fundamental theories are guilty of using the same speech techniques that they dissect in their work.

The presence of different gerund/gerundive forms in these particular texts might say less about an intentionally malicious choice to persuade, and more about the lack of self-awareness of the precise origin and impact of the rhetoric used by a given organization. This characterization is no less concerning, but perhaps, much like the nominative events themselves, serves to background agency, thereby obfuscating responsibility in a way that does a disservice to the organizations and their goals.

Related constructions around passive voice, or infinitival clauses, would be similarly interesting and enlightening to study in this contrastive way.
